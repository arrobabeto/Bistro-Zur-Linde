# Bistro zur Linde create-blog customization

Project-specific editorial style for Bistro zur Linde (Oftringen / Küngoldingen).
Other clients should start from the tool template, not from this document.

## generate

### Voice and audience

Write as the house voice of a warm Swiss neighbourhood restaurant — hospitable,
confident, never corporate or agency-speak. Address guests with **Sie**. Prefer
Swiss High German (de-CH) spelling and wording where natural (_geniessen_,
_grösser_, _Anlässe_, _herzlich willkommen_).

Tone cues from existing site copy:

- Short, grounded invitation: gut essen, sich wohlfühlen, ankommen und
  verweilen.
- Place and belonging: Lindenpark, Küngoldingen, Oftringen, Region /
  Zofingerland — local without sounding tourist-brochure.
- Kitchen craft over hype: saisonal, ehrlich gekocht, regionale Produkte,
  Schweizer Klassiker with a fine French touch when the briefing supports it.
- Soft close that invites a visit or reservation without hard sell.

Primary audience: locals and regulars (Mittag, Feierabend, Familie, Vereine),
plus guests planning private events in Sääli / Wine Room / Zigarrenlounge.
Write so a lunch guest and an evening diner both feel addressed.

When the briefing names people (e.g. Küchenchef), keep titles and spelling
exactly as provided. Do not invent biographies, quotes, or media mentions.

### Language

The project is German-first and effectively monolingual for guest-facing
content. Write the article for the project’s **primary content locale**. Do not
invent parallel EN/FR versions or bilingual requirements unless the briefing
explicitly asks for them.

### Article length and rhythm

Aim for a readable journal piece, roughly **450–700 words** of body prose
(excluding title/meta), unless the briefing asks shorter.

Typical rhythm (mirror the house journal style):

1. **Opening lead** — 2–4 sentences, atmospheric but concrete; sets season,
   place, or occasion.
2. **Two to three body sections** — each with a clear H2 and one or two short
   paragraphs.
3. **Optional pull quote** — one short sentence attributed only if the
   briefing supplies the quote and speaker.
4. **Closing** — warm invitation to visit, taste the season, or enquire about
   a space; no invented promotions.

Prefer short paragraphs. Avoid bullet walls and FAQ blocks unless the briefing
asks for them — the live journal layout is essay-like, not Q&A.

### Heading style

- **Title**: one clear line, informative and inviting (example pattern:
  _Saisonale Menükarte: Herbstgenuss in der Linde_). Title case / sentence
  case in German as natural; no clickbait, no ALL CAPS.
- **H2s**: short, concrete, often noun phrases or gentle imperatives of place
  (_Was die Herbstkarte erwartet_, _Regionale Zutaten im Fokus_, _Ein Menü zum
  Verweilen_). Avoid numbered SEO stuffing (_10 Gründe…_).
- Optional **category** label when fitting: existing journal cues include
  Gastronomie, Der Ort, Weinkultur, Events — pick one that matches the topic;
  do not invent trendy English category names.

Meta / lead: one or two sentences summarizing the piece for listings; same
voice as the body, no keyword stuffing.

### Typical topics (stay inside briefing)

Safe topic families already present on the site:

- Seasonal menus and kitchen stories
- Regional produce and producers (only if named or generically framed)
- Rooms and occasions: Bistro, Sääli, Wine Room, Zigarrenlounge, Terrasse
- Place / Lindenpark / Neueröffnung narrative when factual
- Wine culture and pairing in house spaces (no invented cellar awards)
- Events and celebrations (birthday, Firma, Familie) without fabricated dates

If the briefing is thin, stay general and hospitable; do not invent dishes,
dates, prices, or press coverage.

### Claims that must never appear

Do **not** invent or assert:

- Allergens, dietary guarantees, or medical / health claims
- Prices, discounts, or “gratis” offers unless the briefing states them
- Awards, points, or rankings (GaultMillau, Falstaff, “best of…”) unless the
  briefing explicitly confirms the exact claim for this article
- Competitor restaurant names used for comparison or disparagement
- Invented quotes, guest testimonials, or journalist bylines
- Unverifiable superlatives (_das beste Restaurant der Schweiz_, _einzigartig
  weltweit_) — regional pride is fine; absolute ranking is not
- Opening hours, phone numbers, or reservation mechanics that contradict
  current house facts — prefer a soft CTA (“Tisch reservieren”) without
  inventing channels

When in doubt, describe atmosphere and invitation, not credentials.

### Examples of voice (illustrative, not templates to copy)

- Lead energy: seasonal change in Lindenpark / Küngoldingen → kitchen
  creativity → invitation to the new card.
- Body: Swiss classics meet a fine French note; name a dish **only** if the
  briefing provides it.
- Close: come as you are; stay for the evening; enjoy the season.

## prepare_image

### Cover mood

Warm hospitality photography: cream and coffee tones, soft natural or
interior light, calm and inviting — never neon, glossy stock-food clutter, or
cold clinical plating. The site palette leans cream background, deep brown
text, taupe, and copper accents; images should feel at home next to that.

### Composition

- Prefer a single strong subject: plated dish, ingredient still life, dining
  room, terrace, or kitchen moment.
- Generous crop suitable for a wide hero (landscape orientation). Leave quiet
  space; avoid busy collages, text overlays, logos, watermarks, or date badges
  burned into the image.
- Food should look freshly prepared and regional, not ultra-fine-dining foam
  theatre unless the briefing asks for that look.

### Subjects to favour

- Seasonal produce (pilze, Kürbis, greens), composed plates with jus /
  vegetables, set tables, wine shelves / Wine Room wood and glass, terrace
  lounge, bright Gastraum with guests only if faces are generic and
  non-identifiable or clearly stock-safe.

### Subjects to avoid

- Fake Michelin-star props, trophies, score badges
- Competitor branding or recognizable third-party packaging
- Medical / allergen iconography
- Overly dark moody “steakhouse” filters that fight the cream/copper brand
- Generative artifacts (extra cutlery, warped hands, unreadable menus)

### Caption / alt spirit

When writing alt or caption text: factual, calm German — what is shown and
where (e.g. produce ready for the Linde kitchen). No marketing slogans in the
image metadata.
